# The Tokenised Settlement Index in 2026: Stablecoins, Tokenised Deposits, Wholesale CBDC, and Settlement Finality

Tokenised settlement in 2026 is not a contest between one form of digital money and all others. Stablecoins, tokenised deposits, and wholesale CBDC each solve different problems. The index should measure when each settlement asset improves trust, liquidity, finality, programmability, and integration with regulated financial-market infrastructure.

---

> **Executive Summary / Key Takeaways**
>
> - **Stablecoins are now a policy and banking strategy issue.** The Federal Reserve notes that stablecoins have moved from crypto-market infrastructure toward payment and transaction-account competition.
> - **Tokenised deposits preserve bank money.** They extend commercial bank deposits into programmable environments while retaining the bank-customer relationship.
> - **Wholesale CBDC anchors final settlement.** Project Agorá explores tokenised central bank reserves alongside tokenised deposits for cross-border payments.
> - **The UK is using controlled sandboxes.** The Bank of England and FCA Digital Securities Sandbox allows live testing of digital securities under limits.
> - **Settlement finality is the core metric.** Speed is not enough; legal certainty, liquidity, credit risk, operational resilience, and regulatory treatment determine fitness for wholesale use.
>
---

## Why 2026 Is the Year This Index Matters

The Stanford AI Index is useful because it treats a fast-moving technology field as something that can be measured: research output, technical performance, responsible deployment, economics, sector adoption, policy, and public sentiment are brought into a single frame ([Stanford HAI ⧉](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report "The 2026 AI Index Report")). Banks and financial institutions now need the same discipline for infrastructure. Agentic AI, quantum-safe security, cloud native resilience, and wholesale payments are no longer separate innovation tracks; they are converging into one operating model.

The practical question for a bank is not whether each domain is important. It is whether the institution can measure readiness across all of them at the same time. A bank can deploy agentic AI and still be fragile if its cryptography is not migration-ready. It can modernise cloud platforms and still fail if payment data remains unstructured. It can run tokenisation pilots and still create systemic risk if the settlement, liquidity, identity, and audit layers are not designed together.

## The 2026 Index Architecture

| Index Layer | 2026 Direction | Readiness Metric | Risk if Mishandled |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stablecoins** | Use where blockchain-native liquidity and 24/7 settlement matter | Regulatory status, reserve quality, redemption, custody | Run risk and integration gaps |
| **Tokenised deposits** | Use where commercial bank money and client relationships matter | Bank integration, programmability, balance-sheet treatment | Fragmented bank-led networks |
| **Wholesale CBDC** | Use where central bank money and finality are required | Access model, settlement finality, liquidity design | Slow policy alignment |
| **Digital securities** | Connect issuance, trading, settlement, collateral, and custody | DvP and lifecycle support | Tokenised asset without cash-leg clarity |
| **Legal finality** | Ensure settlement rules work across jurisdictions and infrastructures | Finality opinion and rulebook maturity | Technology without enforceable outcome |

## Three Forms of Digital Money

The index should not treat stablecoins, tokenised deposits, and wholesale CBDC as interchangeable. Stablecoins are often blockchain-native and externally issued. Tokenised deposits preserve commercial-bank money inside regulated banking relationships. Wholesale CBDC or tokenised central bank reserves provide the safest settlement asset for systemic use.

## The Cash Leg Problem

Tokenised securities only become useful at scale when the cash leg is equally robust. A digital bond settled against a manual or delayed cash leg does not deliver the full benefit. The index should measure delivery-versus-payment capability, settlement finality, liquidity impact, and legal certainty.

## From Sandbox to Production

Sandboxes are valuable because they allow live testing under limits. The transition problem is whether governance, custody, resilience, settlement rules, and interoperability survive outside controlled conditions. The index should track which pilots become reusable infrastructure.

## What This Means by Bank Type

### Global Systemically Important Banks

Global banks should treat this index as an enterprise architecture scorecard. The priority is not another proof of concept; it is evidence that autonomous workflows, cryptographic migration, cloud dependency, and payment modernisation can be governed as a single risk and value system.

### Transaction Banks and Corporate Banks

Transaction banks should focus on wholesale payments, structured data, liquidity, tokenised deposits, and agentic treasury services. The most valuable client proposition is not faster money movement alone; it is explainable, auditable, programmable money movement with fewer investigations and better working-capital visibility.

### Regional Banks

Regional banks should use the index to avoid programme sprawl. They do not need to lead every frontier, but they do need credible positions on AI governance, post-quantum inventory, cloud exit evidence, and payment data readiness.

### Fintechs, PSPs, and Infrastructure Providers

Fintechs and infrastructure providers should align their product roadmaps to measurable bank readiness. The best propositions will reduce integration risk, strengthen evidence, and make complex infrastructure easier for banks to govern.

## Conclusion

The value of an index-style report is that it converts a fragmented technology agenda into a measurable operating model. In 2026, the winners in financial infrastructure will not be the institutions with the most pilots. They will be the institutions that can prove readiness across autonomy, security, resilience, settlement, economics, and governance at the same time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do stablecoins replace bank deposits?**

Not necessarily. Stablecoins can compete for some payment flows and transaction balances, but tokenised deposits preserve commercial bank money in programmable form.

**Why does wholesale CBDC matter?**

Wholesale CBDC or tokenised reserves can provide final settlement in central bank money, reducing settlement and counterparty risk for systemic flows.

**What is the biggest settlement-design risk?**

The biggest risk is building a tokenised asset market without a clear, regulated, liquid, and legally final cash leg.

**How should banks compare settlement assets?**

Compare them by finality, regulatory treatment, liquidity, credit risk, programmability, integration, custody, and operational resilience.
