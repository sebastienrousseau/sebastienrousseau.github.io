<section class="qa" aria-labelledby="qa-heading"><header class="qa-head"><h2 id="qa-heading" class="qa-headline">Start in under a minute. <span class="qa-headline-soft">No account, no key, no lock-in.</span></h2></header><div class="qa-list">

<div class="qa-item">
<p class="qa-q">How do I try it right now?</p>
<p class="qa-a">Run the gateway with no install and no account: <code>uvx --from "iso20022-mcp[all]" iso20022-mcp</code>. Ask it to <em>search "cancel a payment"</em> and it points you at camt.056; ask it to <em>generate</em> and you get XSD-valid XML back. Under sixty seconds from nothing to a validated message.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-item">
<p class="qa-q">Which server do I need?</p>
<p class="qa-a">Start with the gateway (<code>iso20022-mcp</code>) and let it route — or install just the one for the job: <strong>initiate</strong> a payment with pain001-mcp, <strong>settle</strong> interbank with pacs008-mcp, <strong>read</strong> statements with camt053-mcp, <strong>reconcile</strong> with reconcile-mcp, <strong>cancel/resolve</strong> with camt-exceptions, <strong>bridge</strong> an agent mandate with ap2-iso20022.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-item">
<p class="qa-q">Is it safe to give an agent?</p>
<p class="qa-a">Yes, by design. Every generator validates against the official bundled XSD before returning; the AP2 bridge only transforms and validates and <strong>never moves money</strong>; and reconcile-mcp is read-only matching. Producing a message is deliberately separate from sending it, so the money-movement step stays a human-guarded action. Apache-2.0, 100% branch-tested.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-item">
<p class="qa-q">Why is this different from a banking API?</p>
<p class="qa-a">Banking platforms sell you an account and their rails. This is the <strong>vendor-neutral message layer</strong> underneath any bank — the ISO 20022 engine, reconciliation and exceptions that every institution still has to build. It runs on your own infrastructure and is tied to no balance sheet.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-item">
<p class="qa-q">Does it help with the ISO 20022 migration?</p>
<p class="qa-a">Directly. MT retires between 2025 and 2028, and structured addresses become mandatory in November 2026. The suite ships the MT→MX converters (MT103/MT101/MT94x) and the structured-address classify/validate/repair/batch toolkit, so you can migrate one message at a time.</p>
</div>
